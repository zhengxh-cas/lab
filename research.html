Bacterial biofilm development

Within bacterial biofilms, individual cells build highly structured three-dimensional communities by responding to external environmental signals, adopting heterogeneous behaviors, and coordinating different modes of motility. We focuse on the molecular mechanisms underlying single-cell heterogeneous activation of second-messenger signaling pathways (c-di-GMP and cAMP) across the stages of biofilm development, dissecting their roles in antibiotic resistance and pathogenesis, and developing anti-biofilm strategies aimed at these environmental-response mechanisms.

Cell envelope stress response

The bacterial cell envelope, composed of cell wall, cell membrane, and lipopolysaccharide, is both the primary interface for sensing the environment and the target of many antibiotics. We focus on sub-inhibitory effects of antibiotics on cell envelope, and dissect the stress response triggered by these damages. We investigate the coordination of central metabolism, cell envelope biosynthesis, and biofilm formation, and explore new antibiotic combination strategies.

Biofilm-host interactions

The phenotypic heterogeneity within a biofilm community means its interaction with the immune system plays out very differently than that of a single cell. We combine human immune cell and animal infection models to understand the physiological decisions made by different subpopulations within biofilms under immune pressure, together with the host's antibacterial response mechanisms. We aim to provide insight into developing new antimicrobial strategies.

How we work

Live-cell confocal microscopy

Time-lapse imaging of growing biofilms with fluorescent reporters, tracking and quantitative analysis of single cell activity.

Bacterial physiology, genetics, and biochemistry

Understanding the molecular mechanisms at the level of individual cells, genes, and proteins.

Multi-omics analysis

Utilizing and developing methods for whole-genome screening, multi-omics characterization, and evolutionary perceptions, deciphering biological consequences from a top-down view.

Infection models

Infection of human cells and animal models to understand the three-way interactions of host, bacteria, and the environment.
